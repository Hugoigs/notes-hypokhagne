
# Le Commonwealth 


"Commonwealth"  -> first qualified : a ==body political== founded on the law for common "well" (welfare)
- It then became used by the philosophers (such as Thomas Hobbes or John Locke ) to mean an ==organized political community== 

In English history : "Commonwealth" refers to the ==republic== established by Parliament and led by ==Olivier Cromwell after the execution of King Charles I in 1649== and continuing until the Restoration in 1660. 
In the United States, the word applied to the religious communities created by the Puritains in Massachusetts. 
- Today Kentucky / Massachusetts / Virginia are still legally known individually as Commonwealths. 

Today's most commun usage of the word Commonwealth refers to ==the informal voluntary association of 56 independent countries== (the most are former colonies of the UK), which formed the ==British Empire of the 19th==. 

In 1884, Roseberry called the Empire a "Commonwealth of Nations " (first time used the named Commonwealth)
In 1887, the first colonial conference led imperial Conferences between the UK and the Prime Ministers of the self-governing dominions. 
On 15 May 1917, ==Jan Smuts==, representing South Africa in the Imperial War Cabinet of World War I. 
At Imperial Conférence of 1926 : prime ministers adopted ==the Balfour Report==, which defined the U.K and Dominions as
- equal in status 
- in no way subordinate one to another in any aspect of their domestic or external affairs
- though allegiance to the Crown 
The 1930 conference ==paved the way== for ==Statute of Westminster== of 1931, in which dominions of : Australia / Canada / Ireland / Newfoundland/ New Zealand and south Africa were described as "==autonomous communities within British Empire=="
As other ==British dependencies gained independence==, come of them becoming republics, the Commonwealth dropped  the requirement that members ==hold allegiance to the British Monarch==, as the symbolic head of their free association.
In non-Commonwealth countries the sovereign is represented by an ambassador =/= In Commonwealth countries, the sovereign representatives bear the name of high Commissionner. 
The Commonwealth nations are : Great Britain / Australia / India / Guyana / Malaysia / Canada / Malta / Pakistan / Nauru / New Zealand / Uganda / Singapore / Maldives ....
- Thirty-one of them have a ==president== and six a ==king== or a ==sultan==, while Queen Elizabeth II was the head of sixteens nations. 
- Commonwealth countries cover about ==a quarter== of the surface of the earths's surface +  spread over every continent and ocean (from Africa to Asia, from Pacific shore to the Caribbean, from in developed  and developing nations) 
- The Commonwealth's 1,8 billion people make up 30% of the world's population. 
- There are many faiths, races, language and culture. 
- India, with ==over 900 million inhabitants==, has more population than all the other Commonwealth nations put together
- Nauru (pop 11,000) and Tuvalu (pop, 12, 000) situated in the Pacific Ocean are two of the smallest nations in the World. 
- Cameroon, thought not a former British colony, was accepted into the commonwealth in 1995.

On various occasions some countries have left the Commonwealth for political reasons :
- In 1962 : South Africa withdrew over the issue of apartheid
- In 1972 : Pakistan withdrew over the recognition of Bangladesh (it rejoined in 1989)
- In 1987 : Fiji left after military coup (it rejoined in 1997)
Some countries gave also been suspended for violating the principles of the ==1991 Harare Declaration== emphazing democracy / human rights and equality. 
- Nigeria was suspended from 1995-1999
- Pakistan's suspension after the 1999 overthrow of a democratically elected government 
- Zimbabwe's president, ==Robert Mugabe==, confirmed in 2004 that he would withdraw from the Commonwealth after his country had been suspended in 2002 

The Commonwealth Secretariat, set up in 1965, is located at Marlborough House in London.
The secretariat staff comes from member countries which also finance it proportion to their GNP.
The present Commonwealth Secretary-General is ==Patricia Scotland==, from Dominica, was elected for a four-year term in 2016 but her term was extended until 2022 due to the COVID epidemic. In June 2022, she was re-elected for a two-years term. 

Commonwealth Heads of Government Meetings have been held regularly since 1944 when they replaced imperial conferences. 
The meetings are held ==in camera== with a view to encouraging a free exchange of ideas, and decisions are normally reached by consensus. 
Each meeting includes a "==Retreat==" to discuss world and Commonwealth issues at the highest level of government.
After each meetings they publish declarations or statements
- The 1971 Singapore Declaration of Commonwealth principles defined the voluntary character, consensual working methods, and goals and objectives of the association.
- The Millbrook commonwealth Action Programme of 1995 reinforced the 1991 Harare (Zimbabwe) Declaration that promoted democracy, good governance, human rights and the rule of law, gender equality, and environmentally sustainable economic and social development.

The Commonwealth games or the British Empire games, staged every four years (half way between two regular Olympic Games).
Every meeting includes athletics and swimming competitions + another eights sport that have included = archery / bowls / boxing...
Although the UK is one member of the Commonwealth : England, Wales, Scotland / Northern Ireland ... take part separately

The Game have been held in Britain on six occasions : 
- London 1934 
- Cardiff 1958
- Glasgow 2014
The games have been marred by international politics
