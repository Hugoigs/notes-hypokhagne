First English settlers in the New World in ==1620== 
-> Britain and America have been united a common language and a similar cultures.

They have founded an instrument for solidarity which Churchill named the "special relationship" in a speech made on ==March 5th 1946 at Westminster College== 
-> He also used the expression "iron curtain" to qualify the Cold war, a particular period. It was a invisible curtain that separated the U.A and part Western Europe from the URSS and part of Eastern Europe. 

On ==September 11th, 1939==, President Franklin Roosevelt wrote a personal letter to Winston Churchill.
->This letter began a friendly relationship between the two leaders.

Their relationship led to a military alliance and a special bond between the US and Britain.

In ==August 1941==, they signed the Atlantic Charter.
->The Atlantic Charter included eight points expressing their shared hopes for a better world.

In ==December 1941==
->They signed the Anglo-American Alliance.
->They created the Combined Chiefs of Staff, a joint British-American military command.

Fifteen days after Pearl Harbor (==December 7th, 1941==), Churchill went to the White House.
-> He stayed there for three weeks and worked daily with President Roosevelt.
-> They planned a common military strategy and shared munitions.
-> They created a joint command in the Pacific.
-> They planned the transfer of US troops to Northern Ireland.

On ==New Year’s Day 1942==, they signed the Declaration of the United Nations.
->The declaration was signed with representatives of the USSR and China.
->They promised to use all their resources to defeat the Axis powers.

The ==Lend-Lease Act of 1941== allowed the USA to supply war materials to allied nations.
->These supplies included food, machinery, and services.

In 1942, Churchill shared nuclear research (Tube Alloys) with Roosevelt.
->Britain helped with the Manhattan Project.

In ==1943==, the Quebec Agreement formalized nuclear cooperation.

The Hyde Park Agreement confirmed continued US–UK cooperation.
->This cooperation was for military and commercial purposes after Japan’s defeat.

At the ==1945 Yalta Conference==, Roosevelt and Churchill worked together with Stalin.
->Relations became tense after Churchill spoke about the “Iron Curtain.”
->The USA hoped for better cooperation with the Soviet Union.

In ==1946==, the first United Nations General Assembly took place.

The USA pressured Britain to allow 100,000 Jews into Palestine.

The United States also replaced Britain in opposing communism in Greece.

In ==April 1947==, the Marshall Plan was launched. 
->It helped repair tensions between the USA and Britain.
->These tensions were caused by the end of Lend-Lease and the McMahon Act.( which deprived Britain of access to US atomic secrets and led Britain to start developing its own nuclear research program.)

==After 1945==: American military bases were established on British soil as part of the transatlantic alliance.
->==1947==: A top-secret UK–USA agreement created COMINT (communications interception).
-> This led to the development of the Echelon system, based notably in Yorkshire, monitoring global electronic communications (civilian and military) for GCHQ and the NSA.

==1948 – Berlin Blockade==: Britain accepted the deployment of US B-29 bombers, capable of carrying atomic bombs, on its bases.
->RAF pilots joined the Americans in the Berlin Airlift, delivering food and medical supplies.
->Several British radar sites became part of the US Ballistic Missile Early Warning System (BMEWS).

==1950 – Korean War==: Britain fought alongside the US against communist expansion and supported NATO’s policy of resisting communism.

Britain worked to become an **atomic power**, and the **special relationship** with the US remained strong despite British nuclear scientists spying for the USSR.

==1956 – Suez Crisis==: The Anglo-French attack on the Suez Canal was strongly condemned by President Eisenhower, forcing Britain to withdraw and highlighting its dependence on the US.

==March 1957 – Bermuda Conference==: Britain was allowed to receive **US-made Thor intermediate-range ballistic missiles (IRBMs)**.
->The **McMahon Act** was amended to allow closer US–UK nuclear cooperation.

After the Soviet launch of ==Sputnik (1957)==, Eisenhower and Macmillan expanded collaboration in defence research and development.

This led to renewed exchange of nuclear weapons technology, including joint weapons testing and shared data and designs.
->Under President Kennedy, Britain allowed the installation of US missile submarines in Scotland.

 Britain supported the US during the ==1962 Cuban Missile Crisis==.

==1962 Nassau Conference & 1963 Polaris Sales Agreement==: British submarines were equipped with **Polaris missiles** fitted with **British warheads**.

==October 1973 – Yom Kippur War==: Tensions arose due to **different US and UK positions on Israel**.

Britain **refused to support** the US boycott of the ==1980 Moscow Olympic Games==.

==1982 Falklands War==: The US **supported Britain** by supplying missiles to bolster air defenses.

==The Thatcher government== : Britain allowed the **US to deploy Cruise missiles** on its soil, sparking **Greenham Common protests**.

==Reagan presidency (1980–84)==: Additional Cruise missiles were installed; Britain served as a **base for the 1986 air raid on Libya** with Thatcher’s approval.

==Thatcher–Reagan period==: Marked a **revival of close US–UK cooperation**, driven by aligned views on the **Cold War**and **economic reform**.

==Post-9/11 (2001)==: President Bush called Britain **“America’s truest friend”**
->Britain deployed **military forces to Afghanistan**, including a **task force and SAS regiment**.

During the ==First Gulf War== British troops fought alongside Americans and were also deployed in Iraq.

The **“special relationship”** often relied on **personal ties**: ==Churchill–Roosevelt, Macmillan–Kennedy, Thatcher–Reagan, Blair–Clinton, Blair–Bush.==

Personal friendship did **not prevent disagreements**:
-> **Eisenhower** forced Britain out of Egypt after the **Suez-Sinai War**.

->**Harold Wilson** refused to join the US in the **Vietnam War**.

-> ==1983 US invasion of Grenada== strained the relationship.

==Economic tie==: By 2004, the US was the **largest investor in the UK** and vice versa, creating **mutual economic benefits**.

==Cultural and educational links==:
->Clinton and advisers studied at **Oxford**.
->Many of Blair’s advisers studied at **MIT or Harvard**.
->Influenced **American-style political reforms** in Britain and preference for **free-market policies** over the European social model.


==June 2005 – Bush–Blair meeting== : Coordinated the **G8 summit** and partnership.
-> Points of tension: Diverging views on the **Middle East**, **China arms embargo**, **Iran’s nuclear program**, and **Kyoto Protocol**.
==July 7, 2005 London bombings==: Strengthened **US–UK cooperation against terrorism** through **military support, diplomacy, and intelligence sharing**.

==May 2011 – Obama at Buckingham Palace==:
->Obama emphasized **friendship, solidarity, and closeness**.
->The Queen highlighted the **“special relationship”** and strong bilateral links.

==British influence== : UK opposition prevented **US–UK military strikes on Syria**, showing that public and parliamentary input affected joint decisions.

==Five Eyes” network==: UK, US, Canada, Australia, and New Zealand **share critical intelligence**.

==1982 Falklands War==: US provided **satellite data and intelligence** to Britain.

->Echelon system (RAF Menwith Hill, North Yorkshire)**: Can **intercept phones, faxes, emails, and internet communications**; only **one-third of 2,200 staff are British**.

==June 2013 – Edward Snowden leaks==:
->Leaked **50,000–200,000 classified documents** revealing **global NSA/GCHQ surveillance**, including business and political communications.
-> Demonstrated the **close intelligence cooperation** and the **intelligence dimension of the “special relationship”**.

==January 2018 – Jeremy Corbyn==:
-> Dismissed the **special relationship** as less relevant today.
-> Emphasized **Britain’s global trading relationships** with the EU, India, China, and others.

==US–UK perceptions==:
->The **US pays less attention to Britain** than vice versa.
-> Media discussion of the special relationship **resurfaces with each new US president**.

==2021 – Liz Truss==:
->Noted **trade with Canada, Japan, Mexico** was better than with the US.
->Mentioned **ongoing disputes** like steel tariffs.
->UK and US **coordinated closely on Ukraine**, sharing and declassifying intelligence.

==Overall trend==:
->The **special relationship has ebbed and flowed**, but **no leader wants to be seen as ending it**.
