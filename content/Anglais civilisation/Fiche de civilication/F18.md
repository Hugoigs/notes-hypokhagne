## England 

=="Welfare"==  refers to aid and monies given to a person or community by the State rather than the private sector 

In Elizabeth I's reign , provisions existed for the care the poor : 
- In 1572 : a law helped the impotent poor and punished healthy beggar 
- In 1601 : ==a Poor law== created taxation to fund relief activities and provision to make the poor work *
- 1662 : ==Settlement and Removal Act== gave a poor newcomer the right to relief in any parish where he managed to live unchallenged for 40 days 
- 1722 : ==Workhouse Test Act== embodied 2 principles : 
	- The prospect of the workhouse should act as a deterrent 
	- relief should be available only to those desperate enough to accept its regime 
- 1782 / 1834 : ==Poor relief Act== / ==Poor law== worsened the position of pauper 
- 1911 : Lloyed George introduced a ==National Insurance Act== providing unemployment insurance 
- 1918 : Ministry of Health took over the care of the poor 
- 1925 : ==Widows, Orphans and Old Age Contributory Act== created an insurance system and 
- 1934 : The ==Employment Act== was set up 


The 1942 ==Beveridge Report on social Security== identified 5 evils : 
- ==ignorance / illness / disease / squalor / want==
-> So launched the idea of a welfare state to help people from " cradle to grave"
To ensure a minimum standard of living for all : it recommended
- a national health service / social insurance / family allowances / full-employment policies 
-> Financed by a ==5%== National Insurance Contribution paid by all working people. 
-> The benefits would be paid to the unemployed, the sick, the retired and the widowed. 

- The 1944 ==Education Act== raised the school-leaving to 15 with universal free schooling. 
- The 1945 ==Family Allowance Act== provided a regular sum to help destitute families 
- 1945 : (Labour Party's Victory) LB passed the ==National Insurance Act (NIS)== to implement the Beveridge scheme. -> Funded by employers / employees / the government 
- The ==National Health Service (NHS) Act==  to institute a free health service 
- 1948 : the ==National Assistance Act== abolished the ==Poor Law== 

Under the ==NHS Act== : 
-> All hospitals were nationalised => hospital doctor / nurses /  staff became salaried personnel of the State 
-> People would register with General Practitioner (GP) salaried by the NHS enjoy free diagnosis and treatment, at home or in hospital.
-> Visits to the GP, who refers patients to hospital consultant if need be, are still free, with a set charge of £6,50 for each prescription. 
-> NHS spending rose from ==£43 bn in 2000 to £70bn in 2005== 
-> It is often criticized for long delay, in March 2007 ==126,200 patients== had been waiting for operations for over 6 months
-> Since 2002 : Patients have been allowed to be operated on by private providers or to abroad : 
- For hip and knee replacement 
- cataract and routine surgery 
=> with expenses paid by the NHS


Numerous benefits exist
____
- Income support for low-income people is means-tested
- Family Credit, or Working Families Tax Credit since 1999, provide a minimum income 
- No Family with children pays income tax if they earn under ==£235 a week==
- Means-tested Housing Benefits help some pay their rent 
- The ==Jobseeker's Allowance== (JSA) is paid for 6 months to the unemployed who are actively seeking work
	-> JSA is not longer means-tested, however, if you receive an income of ==over 50%== a week from an occupational or personal pensions -> the excess will be deducted from your new style JSA 
- Since April 2022 : you can get a personal allowance each week of up to ==£61.05== if you're 18 to 24, and ==£77.00==, if you're 25 or over 
- ==Statutory Maternity Pay== (SMP) is paid for up to ==39 weeks== 
	- You get ==90%== of your average weekly earning (before tax) for the first ==6 weeks== 
	- ==£156.66 or 90%== of your average weekly earnings (whichever is lower) for the next 33 weeks 
	-> To qualify for SMP, you must have been earning on average an amount equal to the lower limit (LEL) of ==£123== a week 
- Child Benefit is payable to families with children, based on the number of eligible children in the household 
	- From April 2022, it is ==£21.80== per week for oldest child / ==£14.45== per week for subsequent children 
- Employees receive Statutory Pay (SSP) = ==£99.35== per week for a maximum of ==28== weeks
- Retirement pension age 
	- for both men and women is currently ==66== years old -> but it will go on increasing after ==May 2026== 
	- The full rate of the new State Pension : About ==£195== per week (in 2021-22) -> it is expected to change, depending on the number of qualifying years (35 years normally)
	- It can be supplemented by the ==Additional State Pension== (State-Related Pension Scheme (SERPS)) => It is an extra amount of money for : man born before ==6 April 1951== / Woman born before ==6 April 1953== 
- A widowed parent's allowance is paid to any parent to any parents bringing up children

National Health Service for foreigners 
___
In December 2013 : In view of the number of people who came to the UK to get NHS care without paying, the government announced that : 
- foreigners would be charged for emergency treatment on the NHS as part of a clampdown on health tourism 
- would have to prove they are not foreigners to get free NHS treatment.

In October 2021 : It was estimated that health tourism drained the NHS of at least ==£300-400 millions== each year 
- It is claimed that => the NHS has become an "==international health service=="
- including of maternity service => a total cost of ==over 5 billion== pounds a year  : around ==50.000 women== giving birth in the UK are either temporary migrants or visitors, which cost taxpayers ==£182 million== a year. -> Thought they are charged after receiving NHS care, only a fraction of cost is paid (hospitals fail to identify overseas patient or never send them bills)

The Health Secretary plans to charge patients from outside the EU one-and-a-half times the cost of the treatment, to be paid in advance 
=> however everyone will remain entitled to emergency treatment because of the ==Human Rights Act==
- To prevent risks to public health such as HIV / STDs / tuberculosis 
- GP and nurse consultations are expected to remain free

## USA 

==Social security== : refers to a system of social insurance designed to pay workers a continuing income after retirement. 
- It must be distinguished from help give, to ensure access to medical treatment 

English Pilgrim brought with them the tradition of community help towards the poor and (people felt obliged to aid) the needy : 
- In the 18th and 19th centuries -> most poverty relief provided in almshouses was made unpleasant to 'discourage" dependency. 
The development of industry and urbanization -> caused the disappearance of extend family + the increase in life expectancy led some States to experiment ==with old-age assistance== or ==launch cash allowances==

- 1935 : The Congress passed a ==Social Security Act== (OASDI) as part of President Roosevelts's New Deal program :
	- It established a federal system of old-age benefits for workers only 
	- A Federal-State system of unemployment insurance 
	- It created a federal grants to the States for Old-Age Assistance / Aid the Blind / the maternal and child services program (later this Aid to Families with Dependent Children (AFDC)/ Temporary Assistance for Needy Families (TANF) after 1996)
- 1939 : Congress added benefits for dependents 
	- Making the Old-Age Insurance system a family security 
Various amendements enlarged its scope : 
- 1956 : Disability Insurance 
- 1964 : Food Stamp Program (now the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP))
	-  On average, SNAP participants received an estimated ==$127== per month per peson 
	- It provides benefits to eligible low-income individuals and families via an ==Electronic Benefits Transfer== card to purchase eligible food in authorized retail food stores 
	- To qualify for the SNAP program => a family's income must be at or below ==130%== of the poverty line, which, for fiscal year 2023 is ==$1.920== a month / For a three-person family it is ==$2,495== a month or about ==$29,940== a year 
- 1972 : The creation of delayed retirement credit, which raised benefits for those who leave after Normal Retirement Age (NRA, 67 for people born after 1960)
