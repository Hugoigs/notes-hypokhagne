
Summary – The September 11 Attacks and Their Aftermath

On Tuesday, September 11, 2001, 19 Islamic terrorists linked to al-Qaeda and Osama bin Laden hijacked four commercial airplanes in the United States.
	•	8:46 a.m. – American Airlines Flight 11 crashed into the North Tower of the World Trade Center in New York.
	•	9:03 a.m. – United Airlines Flight 175 hit the South Tower.
	•	9:37 a.m. – American Airlines Flight 77 crashed into the Pentagon near Washington, D.C.
	•	10:03 a.m. – United Airlines Flight 93 crashed in a field in Pennsylvania after passengers tried to regain control. Its probable target was the White House or the Capitol.

Nearly 3,000 people were killed, including passengers, firefighters, police officers and civilians. The Twin Towers collapsed, destroying or damaging surrounding buildings and subway stations.

Rescue and emergency services responded immediately. 344 firefighters and 60 police officers died during rescue operations.

⸻

Political and military response
	•	October 7, 2001 – The United States launched bombings in Afghanistan against al-Qaeda and the Taliban.
	•	October 2001 – The USA PATRIOT Act was passed, increasing surveillance and powers of law enforcement, though criticized for limiting civil liberties.
	•	November 2002 – The Department of Homeland Security (DHS) was created.
	•	A high terror alert system was introduced in 2002.

Zacarias Moussaoui was arrested in August 2001 and later sentenced to life in prison in 2006.

⸻

Reconstruction of Ground Zero
	•	2004 – Initial designs by Daniel Libeskind were later replaced by a new project by David Childs.
	•	April 27, 2006 – Construction of the new tower officially began.
	•	August 30, 2012 – Steel structure completed.
	•	April 2012 – Total cost estimated at $3.9 billion.
	•	May 10, 2013 – The final part of the antenna was installed, bringing One World Trade Center to the symbolic height of 1,776 feet.
	•	November 2014 – The building officially opened to the public.

⸻

Memorials and remembrance
	•	2011 – The National September 11 Memorial was dedicated on the 10th anniversary.
	•	May 2014 – The 9/11 Museum opened to the public.
	•	At the memorial, two square pools with waterfalls stand where the Twin Towers once stood, and the names of the victims are engraved.

Inside the museum:
	•	Artifacts from the attacks are displayed.
	•	In May 2014, the unidentified remains of 1,115 victims were placed beneath the site.

Tribute in Light was created six months after 9/11: two blue beams of light are projected into the sky every September 11 to honor the victims.
